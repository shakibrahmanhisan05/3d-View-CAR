# PART 1 — THE HARD TRUTHS

## 1.1 Your strategy document was written for the wrong country

The `.md` file you uploaded is a good document. It is also a document about India, or the US, or the Gulf. Applied to Chattogram it fails in three specific places, and you need to see exactly where before you build anything.

**Failure 1 — The pricing is roughly 20× too high.**

That plan proposes a "Showroom" tier at $349/month. At today's rate of about ৳123.5 to the dollar, that is **৳43,000 per month, ৳5,17,000 per year.**

Now look at what this market actually pays for software and web work. In Bangladesh in 2026, <cite index="31-1">a basic business website costs ৳8,000–৳25,000 from a freelancer, or ৳20,000–৳50,000 from a proper agency, and e-commerce stores start at ৳40,000 and can climb past ৳1,50,000</cite>. Another local agency survey puts <cite index="28-1">a professional small business website at ৳30,000 to ৳70,000</cite>.

So the number sitting in your prospect's head is **৳20,000 to ৳70,000, one time, for an entire website.** You would be walking in and asking for ৳43,000 *every month*, forever. The meeting ends there. It does not matter how good the demo is.

**Failure 2 — There is no working payment rail for SaaS here.**

Recurring international card billing does not exist for the average Chattogram showroom owner. There is no Stripe. Your customer pays by bKash, Nagad, or a bank transfer, usually after you physically remind him. A subscription business requires frictionless recurring collection. You do not have one. Any plan that assumes monthly auto-billing is fiction for at least a year.

**Failure 3 — and this is the one that matters most —**

## 1.2 You cannot configure a car that only exists once

This is the single most important sentence in this document.

A configurator is a tool for choosing between **options that do not exist yet**. You pick red instead of white, 17-inch instead of 16-inch, and the factory builds that one. It is a tool for the **new vehicle** market.

Bangladesh's car market is not a new vehicle market. On the country's largest vehicle marketplace, <cite index="37-1">51% of users prefer reconditioned cars, 48% prefer used cars, and only 1% prefer new cars</cite>.

Think about what that means for your product. A dealer on Sheikh Mujib Road has a 2019 Toyota Axio on his floor. There is **exactly one of it**. It is pearl white because the previous Japanese owner bought it pearl white in 2019. It has the wheels it has. The buyer cannot choose a colour. The dealer cannot order a different one. The auction sheet is fixed, the grade is fixed, the mileage is fixed.

"Change the paint, pick your alloys, build your own vehicle" is a feature with **no meaning whatsoever** for 99% of the cars sold in this country.

If you walk into Bismillah Car Centre and demo a colour-swapping Mercedes GLS, the owner will be impressed for eleven seconds and then say *"but I don't have that car, and I can't change the colour of the ones I do have."* He will be completely correct, and you will have no answer, and you will lose the deal.

**This is why the Mercedes / Mustang / Audi demo cannot be the whole product.**

## 1.3 So what survives? Three things — and they are enough

The configurator premise is not dead. It is dead **for one segment**. Here is where it is fully alive in Chattogram:

**A. Motorcycles.** New units. Multiple colours per model. Real accessory attach — crash guards, seat covers, exhausts, LED kits, tank pads. A young, mobile-first buyer. Configuration is real here in a way it simply is not for used cars. Bangladesh's motorcycle market is also structurally healthier: <cite index="12-1">over 80% of motorcycles sold in Bangladesh are locally assembled or manufactured, and the market remains structurally strong despite political instability and currency pressure, with the 2025 rebound confirming resilient underlying demand</cite>. Chattogram has a dense strip of bike showrooms on CDA Avenue and around GEC Circle. **This is your beachhead.**

**B. Modification and accessory shops.** For these businesses, visual change *is the product being sold*. "Show me my Axio with these alloys, this wrap, this body kit" is exactly what they are already trying to describe with words and blurry photos. The configurator is not a marketing add-on for them — it is the actual sales tool. Chattogram has these: Fast and Furious BD on CDA Avenue advertises itself as a one-stop car modification shop; Automart and Al Kaz International at Dewanhat sell accessories and premium headlights.

**C. New-brand car showrooms.** KIA, BYD, Hyundai, Proton. These sell **new cars with real trims and real colours**, they are fighting hard for share in a market that has always defaulted to reconditioned Toyota, and they have actual marketing budgets. AUTOTEC-BYD and KIA Motors both have Chattogram showrooms.

**And for the recon car dealers — the biggest, densest cluster in the city — you do not sell a configurator at all.** You sell them something else entirely, which is section 2.3, and which is the best idea in this document.

## 1.5 The market is in a downturn, and you must know this before you walk in

Do not walk into a car showroom in Agrabad talking about growth and opportunity. The owner has had a bad two years and he will think you are a child.

Here is the actual state of the recon car trade: BARVIDA reports that <cite index="4-1">the proposed budget raises the tax burden on popular reconditioned vehicles, with overall tax incidence on the 1,201cc–1,600cc segment rising to 159.80 percent from 132.36 percent, which may push prices of popular models such as the Toyota Premio and Axio up by ৳2.5 lakh to ৳3 lakh</cite>. Those are precisely the cars that move volume. BARVIDA's president has cited <cite index="9-1">rising import costs, a weakening taka, higher consumer loan rates, and stagnant middle-class incomes as key factors behind the market's decline</cite>.

**How this cuts for you — both ways, honestly:**

*Against you:* budgets are tight. Discretionary spending on "nice website" is the first thing cut. Some of these dealers are genuinely struggling.

*For you:* when inventory sits and every walk-in matters more, anything that credibly produces more qualified buyers gets attention it would not get in a boom. And it argues strongly for weighting your effort toward **motorcycles**, which are growing, and away from recon cars, which are contracting.

**Your entire pitch framing follows from this.** You are not selling a beautiful website. You are selling *"the same number of visitors, more of them walking in."* Say it in those terms or do not say it.

# PART 2 — WHAT PHOENIX ACTUALLY SELLS

## 2.1 Stop selling software. Sell a project.

For the next 90 days, Phoenix is **not a SaaS company.** It is a small studio that builds a specific, unusual, visually striking kind of website for vehicle businesses, and shoots the vehicles itself.

That framing is worth money for three reasons: it prices inside a band the market already accepts, it gets paid up front instead of monthly, and it does not require billing infrastructure you do not have.

The SaaS company is what this becomes in month 9 if it works. It is not what you sell in week 3.

## 2.2 The three products

### **P1 — Showroom Site** · ৳45,000–75,000 one time + ৳1,500/month care
The volume product. A fast, bilingual Next.js site: inventory listings the owner can update, WhatsApp lead capture, Google Maps, service booking, and **one hero vehicle in live interactive 3D on the homepage.**

The 3D is what makes ৳60,000 feel reasonable when the shop next door paid ৳30,000 for WordPress. That single differentiator is doing all the pricing work — do not discount it away.

### **P2 — 3D Configurator** · ৳25,000–40,000 per model, sold as an add-on
Colour switching, wheels, accessories, live price total, "send this build to WhatsApp." Sold to bike showrooms, mod shops, and new-brand car dealers. **This is the demo that wins the meeting.** It closes P1 even when the client never buys P2.

### **P3 — 360° Vehicle Capture** · ৳1,200–2,500 per vehicle
Read the next section carefully. This is the most important product you have.

## 2.3 The idea that makes this a real business: Omlan is the moat

Here is the problem nobody in this space has solved for Bangladesh, stated plainly:

> There is no 3D model of the specific 2019 Toyota Axio sitting on the floor at Shamsons Auto. There never will be. No OEM will give you one. You cannot buy one. And that individual car is the entire product being sold.

Every foreign configurator company fails here. Zakeke, ZeroLight, the $29/month generic tools — all of them assume a CAD file exists. For 99% of the Bangladeshi car market, **it doesn't.**

**But you have a professional photographer on your team.**

You do not need a 3D model. You need Omlan, a turntable or a chalk circle on the showroom floor, a tripod, and 32 frames shot at even intervals around the actual car. Stitched into a smooth drag-to-rotate 360° viewer with clickable hotspots — *"scratch on rear bumper, disclosed"*, *"auction grade 4.5"*, *"37,000 km, original"* — on the dealer's own website.

Consider what that is worth to a recon dealer specifically:

- **It replaces the trust problem.** The single biggest friction in reconditioned car sales is the buyer's suspicion that photos hide damage. A 360° spin with the flaws *labelled* is the strongest trust signal that exists in this trade. It is the digital equivalent of the auction sheet — and every recon buyer in Bangladesh already knows what an auction sheet is.
- **It works on the car he actually has.** No licence issues, no OEM permission, no CAD. It is his car, his floor, his lighting.
- **It is inherently recurring.** Every container that lands is 8–15 new vehicles that need shooting. That is a monthly invoice generated by his own business cycle, not by a subscription he has to remember to renew.
- **Nobody in Chattogram is offering it.** Check every dealer site on Sheikh Mujib Road. They are static photo galleries and Facebook albums.

**Economics per vehicle:** roughly 25–35 minutes of Omlan's time to shoot, plus an automated processing pipeline Hisan builds once. Charge ৳1,500. A dealer with 20 units on the floor is a ৳30,000 first invoice and ৳10,000–15,000/month after. Land four recon dealers on that and you have ~৳50,000/month recurring that requires no new software and no subscription billing.

**This is your actual business.** The 3D configurator is the marketing. The capture service is the revenue.

## 2.4 Positioning line

> **Bangla:** আপনার শোরুম রাত ২টায়ও খোলা। ক্রেতা ঘরে বসেই গাড়িটা চারপাশ থেকে দেখে, রং বেছে নেয়, দাম জানে — তারপর সিদ্ধান্ত নিয়ে আপনার কাছে আসে।
>
> **English:** Your showroom, open at 2am. The buyer walks around the vehicle from bed, picks the colour, sees the price — and arrives at your desk already decided.

Lead with the Bangla version in every meeting. Always.

# PART 5 — THE 12 WEEK PLAN

## Weeks 1–3 · Build the weapon

**Goal: one live Phoenix site + one free flagship build that makes a real showroom look world-class.**

**Week 1**
- *Hisan:* Convert the Audi R8 to optimised GLB (Blender → glTF → Draco → KTX2). Get it to **under 4 MB total, loading in under 3 seconds on a mid-range Android over mobile data.** Do not move on until that number is hit. - *Tasfia:* Build the prospect sheet from the tables in Part 3. Add phone numbers, Facebook pages, opening hours. Target 40 businesses.
- *Siam:* Draft the pitch script in Bangla. Write the ROI calculator logic.

**Week 2**
- *Hisan:* Homepage with live 3D hero, configurator page, ROI calculator, lead form → WhatsApp.
- *All:* Choose **one** flagship target. My recommendation: a Tier 1 bike showroom, ideally Twenty Eight Motors or NM Honda. Big enough to be credible, small enough to say yes fast.
- *Tasfia + Omlan:* Walk in. Do not pitch. Say: *"We're building a demo of what a 3D showroom website looks like for a Chattogram bike shop. We want to build it for you, free, this week. No obligation, no payment. We just want one good example to show people. Can we photograph your showroom and your top-selling model?"*

  Almost nobody says no to that. It costs them nothing and flatters them.
- *Omlan:* Shoot their showroom and their bestselling bike.

**Week 3**
- *Hisan:* Build their site. Their logo, their colours, their phone number, their bikes, their prices, live 3D configurator on their bestseller.
- *Deliver it in person.* On a laptop, in their showroom, in front of the owner and his sales staff. Say nothing while they play with it. Watch what they touch.
- *Omlan:* Film the owner's reaction. Film a salesman showing it to a real customer. **That 40-second clip is your single most valuable sales asset for the rest of the year.**
- Ask for exactly one thing in return: a written testimonial and permission to use their name.

**End of week 3 you must have:** Phoenix site live. One real showroom site live. One reaction video. One named reference. If you do not have these, do not proceed to Part 5.2 — finish them first.

## Weeks 4–7 · Sell

**Goal: 20 in-person meetings. 3–5 paid deals.**

This is where student teams die. Weeks 1–3 are fun — building is fun. Week 4 is walking into a stranger's shop and being ignored. **Most teams quietly stop here and go back to "improving the product."** If you catch yourselves adding features in week 5, you are hiding.

**The hard rule: 6 in-person visits per week, minimum, Tasfia + Omlan.**

Walk the Sheikh Mujib Road strip. Eight showrooms in one afternoon. Then CDA Avenue for bikes. Then Dewanhat for accessories.

**The meeting structure — 12 minutes, not 30:**

1. **Minute 0–2 — Diagnose, don't pitch.** *"How do most customers first find you? What do they usually ask before they visit?"* Let him talk. Take notes on paper, visibly. It signals respect.
2. **Minute 2–4 — Reference.** Show the reaction video from your flagship client. Name them. Local names carry more weight in this market than any statistic.
3. **Minute 4–8 — Hand him the phone.** Not the laptop. **His hand, his phone, your configurator.** Say nothing. Let him rotate it, change the colour, tap the accessories. Silence is doing the work.
4. **Minute 8–10 — ROI calculator, filled in with his numbers.** Ask him how many walk-ins a month and his average sale value. Enter them live. Let the arithmetic make the argument, not you.
5. **Minute 10–12 — One ask.** *"We'll build your bestselling model in 3D, free, this week. If you like it, we'll talk about the full site. If you don't, you keep it anyway."*

**Objection handling — the ones you will actually hear:**

| He says | You say |
|---|---|
| *"I already have a Facebook page."* | "Facebook shows your inventory. This shows a customer *his* car. And when he messages you, you get his full spec, not 'price?'" |
| *"Website is expensive, business is slow."* | "This costs less than one month of boosting posts. And it makes that boosting convert better. What are you spending on boosts now?" |
| *"My customers are older, they won't use it."* | "Your customer's son will. He's the one researching at night, and he's the one who tells his father which showroom to visit." |
| *"I sell used cars, I can't change the colour."* | **"Correct — so we don't do that for you. We do this."** [show the 360° capture] "This is *your* actual car. Every scratch labelled. Buyers trust what they can inspect." |
| *"Send me a proposal, I'll think about it."* | "I'll do better — give me three days and I'll build your bestselling model in 3D. Free. Then decide with something real in your hand." |
| *"Who else in Chittagong is using this?"* | Name your flagship client. This is why week 3 exists. |

**Pricing discipline:** first three clients at ৳45,000 in exchange for a named testimonial and a filmed case study. From client four, ৳65,000. From client seven, ৳85,000. **Raise the price every three clients.** If nobody ever pushes back on price, you are too cheap.

**Getting paid:** 50% advance before work starts, no exceptions, no "after delivery" arrangements. Final 50% on handover. **Domain and hosting stay in your account until final payment clears.** Write it in a one-page Bangla agreement, both parties sign, both keep a photo of it. Every unpaid student project in Bangladesh started with a verbal understanding between friendly people.

# PART 7 — WHAT WILL ACTUALLY KILL YOU

Ranked by probability, not drama.

**1. You will build for eight weeks and sell for two. (~70% likely)**
This is the default failure mode for technical founders and it is what will happen unless you actively fight it. Building feels productive; selling feels like rejection. **Countermeasure: after week 3, Hisan gets a hard weekly build budget and Tasfia's visit count is reported at every Monday standup out loud.** Six visits a week or the week failed. No exceptions for exams, weather, or "the demo isn't ready."

**2. Hisan becomes the bottleneck and everything stops. (~50%)**
One developer, multiple clients, a university course load, and other live projects. **Countermeasure: templatise ruthlessly after client two. Sell no more than two builds per month until the template makes a new site a 6-hour job. Turning down work you cannot deliver is a business skill, not a failure.**

**3. The free build gets taken and never converted. (~40%)**
Owner takes the free model, says "very nice," and stops answering. **Countermeasure: the free deliverable is *hosted by you on your subdomain* and it is explicitly a 14-day demo. After 14 days it comes down or it converts. Say this at the moment you offer it, warmly, in Bangla, and put it in writing. Not later.**

**4. You get a "yes" and then never get paid. (~35%)**
Endemic to freelance work in this market. **Countermeasure: 50% advance, always. Credentials held until final payment. One page, signed. If someone refuses a 50% advance on a ৳45,000 project, they were never going to pay the second half either.**

**5. Asset production becomes an unsolvable bottleneck. (~30%)**
You sell six configurators and discover each model takes two weeks. **Countermeasure: only ever sell 3D for models you have already built or can source. Track two numbers religiously: hours per model, and days from order to live. Everything else is opinion.**

**6. Trademark trouble. (~10%, but not zero)**
You are rendering protected vehicle designs and badges. **Countermeasure: on a dealer's own site, they hold the franchise, so it is defensible. On *your* marketing site, keep badge geometry off unlicensed models, verify every downloaded model's licence, and never let a client download the GLB file itself.**

**7. The team fractures over money or credit. (~25%)**
**Countermeasure: agree the split before the first invoice, in writing, in one paragraph. My suggestion for a 90-day sprint: equal four-way split of net, plus ৳500 per completed 360° vehicle shoot to Omlan and ৳500 per closed deal to Tasfia. Delivery-linked, not seniority-linked.**
